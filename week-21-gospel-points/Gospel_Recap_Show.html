The Gospel: 16 Stops

A recap — and a different approach

The Gospel in One Paragraph

This is where we’re going tonight. By the end, this paragraph will be yours.

God made us good, but Adam broke the rule and two deaths followed—his body and his relationship with God. God is a fair judge who can’t ignore sin, and we’re all guilty kids who keep sinning ourselves. We’re stuck in an unfixable relationship with an unpayable debt, and we’re completely hopeless on our own. But God—rich in mercy—had a rescue plan before He ever created us. He sent Jesus, who was born without inherited sin, lived the perfect life we couldn’t, and still had to die as our substitute—the perfect payment. God raised Him from the dead—resurrection—proving the payment was accepted. Now He calls us to repent & believe into Jesus—and everyone who does receives eternal life.

Stops 10–16 • The Solution

Stop 1

God Made…

“God made…” — the world, Adam, everything. Before light, before time, there was God. He chose to create — and everything He made was very good.

“In the beginning, God created the heavens and the earth.”

Genesis 1:1

He Made Adam — Differently

God didn’t create Adam the way He made trees or animals. He breathed life directly into him. Adam was made to know God, walk with God, and talk with God face to face.

“And the Lord God commanded the man, “You may surely eat of every tree of the garden, but of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil you shall not eat, for in the day that you eat of it you shall surely die.””

Genesis 2:16–17
🗣

In Your Own Words

Stop 1 — God Made…

Complete this out loud: “God made…” Tell the group what He made, why it mattered, and what life looked like before sin. 3–4 sentences.

Stop 2

Broken Rule

They knew the rule. They knew the consequence. They broke it anyway. The rule wasn’t to make them miserable — it was to give them a real choice to trust Him. And they chose not to.

“Did God Really Say…?”

The enemy’s first move was to create doubt about God’s word. Maybe the rule isn’t as serious as He said. Maybe God is holding out on you. Every temptation since has started the same way.

“So when the woman saw that the tree was good for food, and that it was a delight to the eyes, and that the tree was to be desired to make one wise, she took of its fruit and ate, and she also gave some to her husband who was with her, and he ate.”

Genesis 3:6

Why This Wasn’t a Small Mistake

They didn’t just break a rule — they rejected God’s authority. They decided they knew better than God. Every sin since then is the same act: choosing our will over His.

🗣

In Your Own Words

Stop 2 — Broken Rule

What was the rule? What did God say would happen? Why does it matter that they knew the consequence and broke it anyway?

Stop 3

Two Deaths

“You shall surely die” meant two things: Adam’s body would eventually die, and his close relationship with God was broken that very day.

Death #1 — The Body

Before the fall, death wasn’t in the design. After the broken rule, decay entered. Bodies now age, break down, and die. We see this every day. This is not how it was supposed to be.

Death #2 — The Relationship

Something changed immediately — before the body ever died. They hid from God. The open, face-to-face relationship they had with Him was broken. That hiding is spiritual death.

“And they heard the sound of the Lord God walking in the garden in the cool of the day, and the man and his wife hid themselves from the presence of the Lord God among the trees of the garden.”

Genesis 3:8

“And you were dead in the trespasses and sins in which you once walked…”

Ephesians 2:1

Paul doesn’t say “sick.” He doesn’t say “wounded.” Dead. Without rescue, spiritual separation from God isn’t just damaged — it’s fatal.

Illustration

Unplugged

A lamp unplugged from the wall still looks like a lamp — shade, bulb, cord. But it has no power. Spiritual death is being cut off from the source of life, while looking completely fine from the outside.

🗣

In Your Own Words

Stop 3 — Two Deaths

Which death happened immediately? Which one comes later? Why are both part of what God warned about when He said “you shall surely die”?

Stop 4

Fair Judge

God must judge fairly — He cannot overlook sin. A God who ignores evil isn’t good. He’s corrupt. A fair judge cannot pretend the crime didn’t happen.

Illustration

The Wrong Kind of Judge

Imagine a judge who lets the man who murdered your family member walk free — because he also coached Little League and donated to charity. We’d call that judge wicked. Fairness requires that sin be dealt with. God is not that judge.

“And just as it is appointed for man to die once, and after that comes judgment…”

Hebrews 9:27

This isn’t a maybe. Every person who has ever lived will stand before a perfectly fair judge. No technicalities. No charm. No good deeds on the other side of the scale.

The Problem for Us

A fair judge is exactly what we want when someone wrongs us. It’s exactly what we don’t want when we’re the ones who broke the rule. And we all broke the rule.

🗣

In Your Own Words

Stop 4 — Fair Judge

Why is it actually good that God is a fair judge? And why does that same fact create a serious problem for us?

Stop 5

Guilty Kids

We don’t just become guilty when we sin personally. We are born guilty — carrying the weight of Adam’s broken rule. Adam was the representative of the whole human race.

Adam Was Our Representative

When a president signs a treaty, the whole country is bound by it. Adam sinned as the head of humanity. Every person born after him enters the world already carrying a broken relationship with God.

“For as by the one man’s disobedience the many were made sinners, so by the one man’s obedience the many will be made righteous.”

Romans 5:19
Illustration

The Inheritance

If your grandfather declared bankruptcy, you might inherit his debts through no action of your own. We inherited Adam’s broken standing before God. The debt came with the birth.

🗣

In Your Own Words

Stop 5 — Guilty Kids

Explain why we are born guilty before we’ve personally done anything wrong. What does it mean that Adam was our representative?

Stop 6

Keep Sinning

It isn’t just Adam’s guilt we carry — it’s our own personal record of every sin we’ve added since. We have fully and willingly joined the rebellion.

We Come Into the World Already Bent

Nobody has to teach a child to be selfish, to lie, or to demand their own way. Those things come naturally. We are born with a nature already turned away from God — and then we act on it.

“…for all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God.”

Romans 3:23

Not “some.” Not “most.” All. The standard is God’s own holiness — and every person falls short.

🗣

In Your Own Words

Stop 6 — Keep Sinning

How is Stop 6 different from Stop 5? What does it mean that it’s not just Adam’s sin — but our own? Why can’t we just decide to stop sinning and fix the problem?

Stop 7

Unfixable Relationship

No good deeds. No church attendance. No being “a good person.” The broken relationship between us and God is not something we have the power to repair from our side.

Why “Being Good” Doesn’t Fix It

People naturally think: “If I do more good than bad, God will accept me.” But that’s not how a broken relationship works. A judge can’t be bribed with good behavior after the crime is committed.

“We have all become like one who is unclean, and all our righteous deeds are like a polluted garment…”

Isaiah 64:6

Even our best efforts — our most sincere good deeds — don’t meet God’s standard. Our goodness is real, but it isn’t clean enough for a perfect court.

Illustration

The Bottomless Canyon

Imagine standing at the edge of a bottomless canyon with no way across. You could jump with everything you have — it would never be enough. No good deed closes the gap. We made it ourselves, and we can’t cross it.

🗣

In Your Own Words

Stop 7 — Unfixable Relationship

Why don’t good deeds fix the relationship with God? What’s wrong with the idea of earning your way back to Him?

Stop 8

Unpayable Debt

Our sin against God is not just a mistake to overlook — it is a debt owed. The payment required is the shedding of blood. And we have nothing that qualifies.

“…and without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness of sins.”

Hebrews 9:22

God set the payment terms. Sin requires blood. The whole Old Testament sacrificial system was one long picture pointing to this truth.

Why We Can’t Pay It

To pay someone else’s debt, you’d have to have no debt of your own. But we’re all guilty. We can’t pay off our debt to God while we’re still adding to it with every sin.

Illustration

Wrong Currency

Imagine you owe a million dollars and you hand over a bag of foreign coins. It doesn’t matter how much you have — it’s the wrong currency entirely. Our good deeds are the wrong currency. God’s court only accepts one kind of payment.

🗣

In Your Own Words

Stop 8 — Unpayable Debt

Explain why sin creates a debt, why payment requires blood, and why we personally can’t make that payment.

Stop 9

Hopeless

Guilty from birth. Keep sinning. Can’t fix the relationship. Can’t pay the debt. Standing before a fair judge with nothing to offer. There is no human solution.

Don’t Rush Past This One

We need to feel the weight of “hopeless” before the rescue makes sense. The gospel doesn’t land the way it should until we’ve honestly sat with the reality that we cannot save ourselves.

“…remember that you were at that time separated from Christ… having no hope and without God in the world.”

Ephesians 2:12

The Problem — In One Paragraph

Nine stops. One paragraph. This is the problem the gospel solves.

God made us good, but Adam broke the rule and two deaths followed—his body and his relationship with God. God is a fair judge who can’t ignore sin, and we’re all guilty kids who keep sinning ourselves. We’re stuck in an unfixable relationship with an unpayable debt, and we’re completely hopeless on our own.

Nine stops. One verdict.

We cannot save ourselves.

Then — two words arrive that change everything.

Stop 10

But God…

Two words. After all the darkness — the guilt, the broken relationship, the debt, the hopelessness — God decides to move. Not because we deserved it. Because He is love.

“But God, being rich in mercy, because of the great love with which he loved us, even when we were dead in our trespasses, made us alive together with Christ — by grace you have been saved.”

Ephesians 2:4–5  ·  ESV

He Moved While We Were Still Dead

God didn’t wait for us to clean ourselves up or find our way back. While we were dead in sin — hiding, broken, guilty — He moved toward us. That’s what makes it grace.

“…but God shows his love for us in that while we were still sinners, Christ died for us.”

Romans 5:8
Illustration

The Paid Debt

Imagine waking up to find that every debt you owed — every one — had been paid off while you slept. Before you asked. Before you even knew someone was willing. That is “But God” in one moment.

🗣

In Your Own Words

Stop 10 — But God…

Why are “But God” the most important pivot in the gospel? What makes grace feel surprising — even shocking?

Stop 11

Rescue Plan

He had a plan before He created man. The rescue wasn’t invented after the fall. God designed it before creation began. He knew we would fall — and already had the answer ready.

“…the Lamb who was slain from the foundation of the world.”

Revelation 13:8

The rescue was written into the plan before the wreck happened. This was never an emergency response.

The First Hint — Genesis 3

In the same chapter as the fall — the very day everything broke — God gave the first hint of the plan. He told the serpent that a son of Eve would one day crush his head. The rescue begins in the wreckage.

🗣

In Your Own Words

Stop 11 — Rescue Plan

What does it mean that the rescue plan existed before creation? Why does it matter that God planned it rather than reacted to the fall?

Stop 12

Jesus Life / Death

The life came first — and it mattered just as much. Jesus had to live the perfect life we owed God before He could die the death we deserved.

Why the Virgin Birth?

Jesus was born of a virgin not as a symbol, but for a specific reason. Without a human father, He didn’t inherit Adam’s sin guilt. He entered life with a clean record. That was the plan.

“The angel answered her, “The Holy Spirit will come upon you, and the power of the Most High will overshadow you; therefore the child to be born will be called holy — the Son of God.””

Luke 1:35

The Life We Couldn’t Live

For thirty-three years, Jesus faced every temptation we face — hunger, rejection, betrayal, loneliness — and never sinned once. He lived the perfect life that we owe God but cannot produce.

“For we do not have a high priest who is unable to sympathize with our weaknesses, but one who in every respect has been tempted as we are, yet without sin.”

Hebrews 4:15

But the Life Alone Wasn’t Enough

This is the part people miss.

A perfect life qualified Jesus to be the sacrifice. But to actually pay for our sins, He still had to die. The life gave Him the right to be our substitute. The death was the payment.

“When Jesus had received the sour wine, he said, “It is finished,” and he bowed his head and gave up his spirit.”

John 19:30

Tetelestai — Paid in Full

“It is finished” is one Greek word: tetelestai. It was stamped on paid bills in the ancient world. Jesus wasn’t just saying it was over. He was declaring: the debt is paid in full.

🗣

In Your Own Words

Stop 12 — Jesus Life / Death

Walk through the whole sequence: virgin birth → no inherited sin → perfect life → still had to die. What did the life accomplish? What did the death accomplish?

Stop 13

Perfect Payment

Only God could satisfy God. No human could do it. No angel could do it. The infinite debt of sin against an infinite God required an infinite sacrifice.

The Great Exchange

At the cross, the most extraordinary transaction in history took place. Jesus took our sin, our guilt, our record — and gave us His righteousness, His perfect standing, His clean record before God.

“For our sake he made him to be sin who knew no sin, so that in him we might become the righteousness of God.”

2 Corinthians 5:21

Justice Was Not Ignored

God didn’t look the other way to save us. He satisfied His own justice to save us. The fair judge didn’t close His eyes — He stepped down from the bench and paid the bill Himself.

🗣

In Your Own Words

Stop 13 — Perfect Payment

Explain why only God could satisfy God. What is the great exchange? Why does it matter that justice was fulfilled — not ignored?

Stop 14

Resurrection

The resurrection shows God accepted the payment. If Jesus stays in the tomb, nothing works. The empty tomb is God’s declaration: the sacrifice was sufficient. The debt is permanently settled.

The Empty Tomb Is a Receipt

When you pay a debt, you get a receipt as proof the payment went through. The resurrection is God’s receipt — proof that the sacrifice Jesus made was fully accepted.

“…who was delivered up for our trespasses and raised for our justification.”

Romans 4:25

This Actually Happened

The resurrection isn’t a metaphor or a feeling. It happened in a body, in a tomb, on a Sunday morning. Over 500 people saw the risen Jesus — and many of them died defending that testimony.

“And if Christ has not been raised, your faith is futile and you are still in your sins.”

1 Corinthians 15:17
🗣

In Your Own Words

Stop 14 — Resurrection

Why is the resurrection proof that God accepted the payment? What would it mean if Jesus had stayed in the tomb? Why is the resurrection not optional theology?

Stop 15

Repent & Believe Into

The rescue has to be personally received. History alone doesn’t save you. That happens through repentance and faith — and both words mean something specific.

What Does “Repent” Mean?

Repent means to turn — not just feel bad, but feel bad enough to actually change direction. You stop walking away from God and turn toward Him. It involves your mind, your will, and your feet.

Remorse vs. Repentance

Feeling bad is not the same as turning around.

You can feel terrible about something and never change direction. True repentance is the will turning — not just the emotions softening. Judas felt remorse. Peter repented. The difference mattered eternally.

““The time is fulfilled, and the kingdom of God is at hand; repent and believe in the gospel.””

Mark 1:15

This was Jesus’ opening message. Not “be good.” Not “try harder.” Repent and believe.

“Believe Into” — Not Just “Believe About”

You can believe about Jesus the same way you believe about George Washington — as historical fact. That is not saving faith. “Believe into” means trusting Him completely with your past, present, and future.

Illustration

The Parachute

Knowing a parachute works is not the same as strapping it on and jumping. “Believe into” is the jump — putting your full weight on Jesus, not just agreeing He exists. The difference between knowing and trusting.

Two Sides of One Turn

Repentance is turning away from sin and self. Faith is turning toward Jesus. You can’t do one without the other. A real turn away is always simultaneously a turn toward.

🗣

In Your Own Words

Stop 15 — Repent & Believe Into

What is the difference between believing about Jesus and believing into Jesus? What is true repentance? Why are both necessary?

Stop 16

Eternal Life

Our bodies die — but our souls live forever in one of two places. Both destinations are real. Both are permanent. Stop 15 is the only thing that determines which one.

Two Destinations — Real and Permanent

Jesus talked more about hell than He did about heaven. Both are real. We don’t get to keep one and discard the other. The soul does not stop existing when the body dies.

“Whoever believes in the Son has eternal life; whoever does not obey the Son shall not see life, but the wrath of God remains on him.”

John 3:36

“For the wages of sin is death, but the free gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord.”

Romans 6:23 — The Full Verse

We read the first half at Stop 8. Now the whole verse. The contrast is everything: what sin earns — and what God gives freely.

Eternal Life Starts Now

Eternal life isn’t something that kicks in at death. Jesus says in John 17:3 that eternal life is knowing God — a relationship that begins the moment you repent and believe. We are already in it.

🗣

In Your Own Words

Stop 16 — Eternal Life

Explain the two destinations. What is the difference between eternal life and simply “living forever”? When does it start?

The Gospel in One Paragraph — All 16 Stops
